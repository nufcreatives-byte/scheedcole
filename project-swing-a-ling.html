S Hotel · Montego Bay · Jamaica

Swing A Ling

An installation celebrating Charlie Ace and Swing A Ling, his mobile record shop, through a recreated vehicle and a portrait sculpture beside a sound system at S Hotel Montego Bay.

Behind the work

Before it became a sculptural installation, Swing A Ling was Charlie Ace’s mobile record shop on the streets of Kingston. Adrian Boot’s photographs from 1973 capture the man, the painted van and the sound system together.

Explore the story

A record shop on the move

Charlie Ace converted a Morris 1000 van into a mobile shop, carrying records directly to customers through Kingston. Its hand-painted signs advertised records, tapes and different styles of music. Swing A Ling was both a place to buy music and a distinctive presence on the street.

More than a record seller

A deejay and recording artist, Charlie Ace began his career in the late 1960s with producer Joe Gibbs. He later recorded with Lee “Scratch” Perry and other Jamaican producers; his work with Perry included Cow Thief Skank. The shop belonged to that wider world of recording, selling and sharing music.

From photograph to installation

Scheed Cole’s project brings the vehicle and Charlie Ace into three dimensions. Music signs and record imagery cover the recreated shop, while the sculptural portrait rests an arm on a speaker stack. The leaning pose and arrangement echo the scene in Boot’s photograph.

The work remembers more than a performer: it gives physical presence to the street sellers, small businesses and sound equipment that connected Jamaican recordings with their listeners.

Reading the archive

Boot’s colour and black-and-white photographs show the shop in its original street setting. Together, they offer a reference for the figure, vehicle and painted displays, while recording a moment in Kingston’s everyday music culture.

Historical background: AllMusic’s Charlie Ace biography and Adrian Boot’s photographic archive and timeline. The colour photograph is published by Contemporary And in its coverage of Dancing the Revolution.
