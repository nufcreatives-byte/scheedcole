The project

History given
physical presence.

At Montego Bay's Legends Circle, sculptural representations of National Heroes Samuel Sharpe and Marcus Garvey, National Heroine Nanny of the Maroons, and music legends Bob Marley and Jimmy Cliff create a public encounter with Jamaican identity.

The works combine Cole's commitment to realism with recycled-material innovation, making cultural memory visible, approachable and part of everyday public life.

Impact on Jamaica

Behind the work

Getting Bob Marley’s real-life height right was part of the brief. The collection then grew one figure at a time, bringing Jamaican history into an everyday public setting.

Explore the story

Portraits as a public encounter

Legends Circle grew from Christopher Issa’s idea of bringing Jamaican figures into the public space outside St James Place, beside S Hotel Montego Bay. The intention was to make cultural history visible in a place where people could stop, look and connect with familiar faces.

Getting Marley’s scale right

The 2019 Gleaner account explains that the hotel obtained permission from Bob Marley’s family on the understanding that his portrait would reflect his real-life height. Cole was approached after the hotel learned of his work through a project in Negril and a newspaper article.

The collection grew one figure at a time

Marcus Garvey and Nanny of the Maroons followed Marley, while Jimmy Cliff also gave permission for his likeness to be included. Samuel Sharpe joined the group even though another monument already stood in Sam Sharpe Square: this portrait offered a different representation of the same historical figure.

Realism and recovered materials

The article identifies Cole’s recycled material as the basis of these portraits. The ambition was not simply to mark a name, but to give each person a lifelike presence through recognisable features and close attention to appearance.

Keeping the credits clear

The wider Legends Circle also includes works by other artists. The metal Usain Bolt sculpture discussed in the 2019 article is credited to German sculptor Diedel Kloever; it should not be confused with Cole’s separate Bolt portrait shown elsewhere in this portfolio.

Based on Paul H. Williams’s “S Hotel Jamaica embracing and promoting the arts”, The Gleaner, 13 November 2019, reproduced in the artist’s dossier. See Press below.

Related project · Kingston · 2022

National Heroes
Emancipation Parade.

A separate commission brought Jamaica’s National Heroes onto the streets of Half Way Tree for the Emancipation Day parade on 1 August 2022. Lifelike portraits surrounded a monumental diamond under Cole’s theme, “We can all be diamond heroes”.
