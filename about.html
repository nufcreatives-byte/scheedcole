About Scheed Cole

Sculpting
Jamaican identity.

Scheed Cole is a Jamaican sculptor and sculptural design engineer creating monumental works, immersive environments and bespoke installations for public, cultural and commercial spaces in Jamaica and internationally.

The practice

Where art meets
engineering.

For Cole, sculpture extends beyond the object itself. His practice brings together realistic form, industrial fabrication, engineering knowledge, applied art and an understanding of architecture to transform ideas into physical experiences.

His work ranges from public monuments and cultural portraiture to hotel installations, museum environments, attractions and ambitious custom commissions.

“It’s not a sculpture. It’s a message.”

Scheed Cole

His story

Built through
curiosity and making.

Trained as an art educator, Cole earned a diploma in Visual Art Education from The Mico Teachers College and spent more than a decade teaching art at secondary level. Long before monumental commissions defined his practice, he was exploring form, materials, props, fabrication and the challenge of making imagined things real.

That path developed into what Cole describes as sculptural design engineering: a multidisciplinary practice that can require him to move between the roles of sculptor, industrial designer, applied artist, fabricator and problem-solver depending on the project.

Today, his work occupies public spaces, hotels, museums and cultural destinations across Jamaica, while his practice is positioned for commissions internationally.

A collaborative practice

Built together.

Behind Scheed’s work is a dedicated core team, joined by artists, fabricators and other skilled professionals according to the needs of each project. The studio also welcomes trainees and volunteers, bringing together different experiences and skills to bring ambitious ideas to life.

Beyond the sculpture

Making skills
matter.

Education remains part of Cole's practice. Through 360 Applied Design and earlier recycling initiatives, he has worked with young people in hands-on disciplines including fibreglass, carpentry, welding, painting and sculpting.

The work connects creativity with practical skills, discipline, employment and environmental thinking — extending the impact of making beyond the finished sculpture itself.

“An artist must be a polymath.”

Scheed Cole

My approach

Making work
that means something.

I have always been drawn to representing the world around me, especially people and animals. Capturing a likeness takes observation, patience and discipline — and seeing someone recognise the subject in a sculpture is deeply rewarding.

For me, art belongs in everyday life. I want my work to celebrate people, preserve cultural memory and create experiences that communities can connect with.

Read more +

My training at The Mico gave me a formal understanding of art and design, building on the curiosity I had from a young age. Over the years, I have remained committed to realistic, representational work and to interpreting the world as I see it.

I also work with discarded materials, giving plastics, Styrofoam and paper a new purpose within my sculptures. These materials are part of my practice, but I do not want them to define everything I create.

Working on public, private and corporate commissions has brought me together with artists and specialists from many disciplines. I value the opportunity to contribute to culture and heritage, serve my community and inspire the next generation.

Scheed Cole
