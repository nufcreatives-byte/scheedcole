Public sculpture · Kingston · 2022

National Heroes
Emancipation Parade.

Lifelike sculptural portraits created for the Emancipation Day parade in Half Way Tree, Kingston, on 1 August 2022.

The project

History
on the move.

Cole brought realistic portraits of Jamaica’s National Heroes together on a float, surrounding a large diamond set above a mountain-like form. He developed the theme “We can all be diamond heroes”.

The installation brought portrait sculpture into a shared public celebration.

Archive photographs reproduced from Scheed Cole’s dossier.

Behind the work

Working with ten other people, Cole faced a demanding deadline. The portraits were conceived as lasting works, with a life beyond the parade.

Explore the process

A late start, a demanding deadline

Cole initially hesitated because the opportunity came close to the proposal deadline and he was already busy. He eventually accepted, drawn by the chance to demonstrate Jamaican craftsmanship through realistic portraiture.

Building the foundation

He worked with ten other people. In his account, progress depended on him completing the initial stages before others could carry out their tasks. About six weeks of concentrated work were needed to meet the deadline.

Beyond a single day

The aim was permanence. Cole wanted the portraits to continue honouring their subjects after the event, rather than become disposable props. When the October 2022 article was published, a permanent home was being sought.

Based on Paul H. Williams’s Gleaner profile of 9 October 2022, included in the artist’s dossier.
